Stride is an AI-powered financial planning tool. I design it, write it, deploy it, and
support the early adopters using it.

It started as a problem I wanted to fix for myself. Budgeting apps are very good at
telling you what you spent last month. Retirement tools project 30 years out on
assumptions nobody can hold in their head. Neither answers the questions people actually
agonise over: can we afford this in two years, what happens to the plan if one income
stops, is this the year or is next year better. I talked to users and looked at the tools
they already had, and that two-to-five year window was the gap. So I started building.

## The stack

- **Front end:** React and TypeScript.
- **Backend and data:** Firebase, with Firestore for storage, Firebase Auth for accounts,
  and Firebase Hosting for deploys.
- **AI:** Gemini powers a conversational tool inside the product.

I picked Firebase on purpose. As the only engineer, every piece of infrastructure I run
myself is time I'm not spending on the product. Managed auth, storage and hosting let me
put that time into the forecasting model and the interface, which are the parts users
actually notice.

## The forecast is the hard part

The core of Stride is the forecast: take a person's current position, their committed
plans, and a goal they are only exploring, and project both paths forward month by month
so they can be compared on the same chart. Getting that right is less about the charting
and more about the model underneath it. Every committed goal, recurring cost and income
change has to land in the right month, and the "what if" path has to share everything with
the committed one except the thing being explored.

## Building with an AI coding partner

I build Stride with Claude Code as my primary coding partner. In practice that means I own
the design and the decisions, break the work into small, reviewable changes, and read every
diff before it lands. The assistant makes me faster at the parts that are mostly typing.
It doesn't replace knowing what the code should do, and it makes reviewing carefully more
important, not less.

> The assistant writes a lot of the keystrokes. I still own every line that ships.

## Where it is now

Stride is live with early adopters. The loop I care about now is the one between what they
actually do in the product and what I build next, so I can stop reasoning about behaviour
from interviews alone.
