Stride is an AI forecasting agent for short-term financial planning. I run product on
it and I build it. An engineer now advises, and I
check direction with them as we go.

It started as a problem I wanted to fix for myself, and after some conversation it
kept going because the discovery turned up something worth building.

## What the research said

I interviewed users about how they plan financially, and ran competitive research
across the tools they already had. The pattern was consistent enough to build on.

Budgeting apps are backward-looking. They are very good at telling you what you spent
last month and close to useless for telling you what happens if you do a thing next
year. Retirement tools go the other way, projecting 30 years out on assumptions nobody
can hold in their head.

Neither one answers the questions people described actually agonising over. Can we
afford this in two years. What happens to the plan if one income stops. Is this the
year or is next year better.

That two-to-five year window is where the real decisions sit, and it was the part
neither category served.

## What I own

I run discovery independently, synthesise the pain points, and prioritise features. The
PRD, user stories, and acceptance criteria are mine. Tickets go into GitHub Projects,
prioritisation happens in Miro.

The habit I care most about is prototyping before committing build time. I put a
working prototype of a flow in front of someone before it becomes a ticket, because it
is much cheaper to find out a flow is wrong at that stage than after it exists. That
discipline matters more now that I am the one building it. The cost of a bad decision
lands on my own week.

## The build

Google ADK and Gemini for the agent, FastAPI on the back end, React on the front.

Writing the tickets and then implementing them has closed a loop I could not see from
one side of it. Scope I would once have written down in a sentence now has to survive
contact with the thing itself, and the estimates I make are better because I am the
one who has to meet them.

> Writing the ticket and then building it is the fastest feedback loop I have found on
> my own judgment.

## Where it is now

Still building. The honest state is that Stride has a defined problem, a validated
gap, a working prototype, and a prioritised backlog I am shipping against. It currently have early adopters,
and the next thing that matters is amplifying it so I can
stop reasoning about behaviour from interviews alone.
