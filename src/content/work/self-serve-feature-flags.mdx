Feature flags are meant to make changes cheap. At Arctic Wolf, changing one meant filing a
ticket and waiting for it to come off a queue. The flag itself took seconds. The process
around it took far longer, and every engineer who needed a flag flipped was blocked on
someone else to do it.

## Why the queue existed

The queue wasn't there by accident. Flags control production behaviour, and a wrong change
can hurt customers. The ticket was a safety mechanism: a person checked the change before it
happened, and the ticket left a record of it afterwards.

So removing the queue meant keeping both of those properties without the person in the
middle.

## Guardrails and an audit trail

I streamlined the flag system into a self-serve tool that any engineer in the company could
use directly. Guardrails replaced the manual check, constraining what a change could do
before it was applied. CloudWatch audit logging replaced the ticket as the record, so every
change was traceable to who made it and when.

With both in place, the queue had nothing left to do. Removing it cut the friction on flag
changes by 70%.

> The goal wasn't fewer controls. It was moving the controls into the tool.

## What I took from it

Most internal process exists because it protects something. The work is figuring out what,
then building that protection into the system so the process can go away. Done that way,
you get speed and safety together rather than trading one for the other.
